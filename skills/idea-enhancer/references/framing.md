# Framing toolkit

Use this to turn a vague idea or problem into something researchable and solvable.

## 1. Restate
- **As given**: the user's words.
- **Idea or problem?** If it's an idea (a solution), work backwards to the problem it solves. If it's a problem, don't jump to a solution yet.

## 2. Who suffers, how much, how often
| Segment | Pain (what goes wrong) | Severity 1–5 | Frequency | Current workaround | Cost of workaround (time/money/risk) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
Pick the **beachhead segment**: highest severity × frequency *and* reachable *and* able to pay (or backed by someone who pays).

## 3. Job-to-be-done
"When **[situation]**, I want to **[motivation]**, so I can **[outcome]**." Include functional, emotional and social jobs.

## 4. Root causes: 5 whys
Ask "why does this happen?" until you reach something actionable. Often the real problem is information, trust, coordination, access or incentives, not technology.

## 5. Constraints & context
Geography, regulation, budget, infrastructure (connectivity, devices, literacy, language), timing, existing players, the user's own skills/assets (if the user described them).

## 6. First-principles check
Strip the problem to fundamentals: what are the irreducible inputs, outputs and physical/economic limits? What would it cost if built from scratch today with current tools?

## 7. Problem statement (one sentence)
**[Segment]** struggles to **[job]** because **[root cause]**, which costs them **[quantified impact]**; today they **[workaround]**, which fails because **[gap]**.

## 8. Success definition
What would make the user say "this changed everything"? Define 1–3 measurable outcomes (e.g. "cut spoilage from 30 % to 10 %", "reply in < 1 min", "save 5 h/week").
